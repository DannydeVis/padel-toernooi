When to choose King of the Court

King of the Court works best for a competitive group that wants the ranking to change visibly every round, not just at the end. It rewards momentum: win on the King's Court and you stay, lose and you're back on the way down within a round or two, which keeps every match meaningful.

It's different from Mexicano, where partners are re-paired by standings but everyone plays on the same set of courts each round. It also differs from Americano and Mixicano, which use a fixed or gender-balanced rotation without any promotion or relegation. If your group wants fixed pairs that stay together instead of individual rotation, Team Mexicano applies the same standings-driven idea to permanent teams.

Frequently asked questions

How does padel King of the Court work?

Courts are ranked from 1 to N, where court 1 is the King's Court. Every round, each court plays one match. On court 1, the winners stay and the losers drop to court 2. On every other court, winners move up a court and losers move down, except on the bottom court, where the losers stay. Players who arrive at a court together always split up and each partner with someone already there.

How many players do you need for padel King of the Court?

You need at least 8 players. PadelBracket sets the number of courts to players divided by 4: 8 players means 2 courts, 12 players means 3 courts, and so on, up to 32 players. Extra players who don't fit rotate through a fair challenger pool.

What happens to players who don't fit on a court?

If the player count isn't an exact multiple of 4, extra players wait in a challenger pool and rotate in on a fair schedule, so nobody sits out twice before everyone else has rested once. PadelBracket tracks who rested least recently and swaps them into the lineup automatically.

Who wins padel King of the Court?

Standings are ranked by final court first, so ending on the King's Court outranks everyone below, and then by total points scored as a tiebreaker. PadelBracket shows an up, down or same arrow next to every player after each round.

What is the golden point?

A padel match can end in a tie, for example 16 to 16 with a 32 point cap. Since King of the Court always needs a winner to decide who promotes, a golden point decides the tie: the next point wins the match and the court.

Can you play King of the Court on a time limit instead of fixed points?

Yes. PadelBracket supports two scoring modes: a fixed point target per match, or a timer per round so every court plays for the same number of minutes and whoever leads when time runs out wins that match.
